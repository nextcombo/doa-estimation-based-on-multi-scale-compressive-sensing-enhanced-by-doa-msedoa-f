# DOA Estimation Based on Multi-Scale Compressive Sensing Enhanced by DOA (MSEDOA) for Automotive Millimeter Wave Radar

Introduction With the continuous development of advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS) and autonomous driving technologies, radar systems in vehicles have become increasingly important. Doppler of arrival (DOA) estimation is a fundamental aspect of radar target positioning, directly affecting the safety of critical functions such as collision avoidance, lane departure warnings, and emergency braking [1]. Traditional DOA estimation methods rely on beamforming techniques that extend Fourier transforms to the spatial domain. However, these methods are limited by the Rayleigh limit, hindering angle superresolution [2]. To overcome these limitations, researchers have developed various subspace-based DOA estimation techniques, with Multisignal Classification (MUSIC) [3] and Rotation Invariant Signal Parameter Estimation Techniques (ESPRIT) [4] being particularly prominent. Although these methods surpass the Rayleigh limit, they are heavily dependent on pre-established models and struggle to provide stable and accurate estimates under low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and limited snapshot conditions. This hinders their practical application in automotive millimeter wave radar systems.
In recent years, data-driven deep learning techniques have gained significant appeal as robust solutions for DOA estimation [5]. These methods utilize supervised learning to automatically discover and extract relevant features on a wide range of datasets, demonstrating enhanced robustness and adaptability across various application scenarios. Deep learning approaches currently used for DOA estimation can broadly be categorized into two types: regression-based methods and multi-label classification strategies [6]. However, regression-based methods inherently face limitations in practical applications due to their reliance on prior knowledge about the number of targets and their inability to adapt to dynamic multi-target scenarios. In contrast, multi-label classification methods, which predict DOA simultaneously for multiple targets, provide superior flexibility and robustness, making them more suitable for complex environments such as automotive radar systems. Despite the inherent flexibility of multi-label classification methods in DOA estimation, they exhibit poorer convergence under limited fast-sampling conditions, leading to unstable estimation results. Moreover, deep learning techniques that rely on the covariance matrix of the received signal as input impose a significant computational burden on automotive radar systems, a challenge that is particularly pronounced in real-time applications [7], [8].
To address the challenges in DOA estimation, we propose a novel end-to-end method that directly inputs raw IQ signals into a deep neural network. Our approach enhances the SE-ResNet architecture with multi-scale feature extraction and channel attention mechanisms, specifically designed to improve accuracy under low SNR and limited fast-sampling conditions. Experimental results demonstrate that our proposed method achieves precise DOA estimation in complex environments even within the constraints of 10 fast-sampling points, significantly outperforming traditional DOA estimation methods.
Conclusion: We propose MSEDOA, a deep learning-based DoA estimation method specifically designed for automotive millimeter-wave radar systems. MSEDOA leverages the squeeze and excitation residual networks with multi-scale feature extraction and channel attention to provide robust and precise DoA estimation under challenging conditions. Experimental results demonstrate that MSEDOA outperforms traditional techniques in providing reliable estimates in complex environments. Future work will explore integrating classification and regression strategies for super-resolution DoA estimation of unknown sources, and evaluate their feasibility for real-time deployment in automotive radars.
